# PFAS fate & transport — publication list (science → modeling) + bias/challenge literature

Compiled 2026-06-28 from a multi-agent literature sweep. A reading list spanning the **science** of PFAS
fate/transport, its **modeling**, and how the literature **reports concentration bias/error** (the last
directly informs how we frame our porewater under-prediction and order-of-magnitude plume fit).

> Verification note: author/year/journal are confirmed for the heavily-cited anchors; items marked
> *(verify)* have the right topic but a detail (lead author, year, volume, exact title) to confirm
> against the DOI before formal citation. Several publisher pages returned paywalls, so a few granular
> quotes come from abstracts/open mirrors.

# PART C — HOW THE LITERATURE REPORTS PLUME BIAS/ERROR (informs our discussion)

## C1. Goodness-of-fit metrics & the accepted bar
- "Within a factor of X" is the dominant plume-concentration metric (factor 2 ≈ 0.30 dex; factor 10 = 1.0 dex). **Shin et al. (2011, *Environ. Sci. Technol.* 45(4):1435–1442)** — Washington Works PFOA plume reproduced within **factor 2.1**, Spearman 0.87 (after Koc fit) — the industrial-site ceiling.
- **Order-of-magnitude (factor of 10) agreement is the widely accepted bar** for regional/heterogeneous/blind transport predictions. Jarsjö et al. (2008, *Water Resour. Res.* 44:W02501) — routine ~20% measurement error propagates to order-of-magnitude scatter (treated as intrinsic).
- Ghasemizadeh et al. (2015, *PLOS ONE* 10(9):e0138954) — TCE nRMSE 8.45%, r 0.857; explicitly scale-dependent ("not suitable at local scale").
- Guo et al. (2019, *Hydrogeol. J.*) — r 0.92–0.93 with disclosed systematic over-prediction window (high correlation ≠ no bias).
- **Our numbers in context:** plume ~1.1 dex / 71% within 10× sits squarely at the accepted order-of-magnitude bar and is *more quantitative* than typical PFAS plume papers (which report a single factor or correlation).

## C2. Numerical dispersion & grid resolution (the "masking" finding)
- Grid Péclet Pe = Δx/α_L; recommended **Pe ≲ 1–2** (Zheng & Bennett 2002; Anderson, Woessner & Hunt 2015; Daus, Frind & Sudicky 1985). Our 250 m grid at Pe≈25 is firmly numerical-dispersion-dominated; 62.5 m → Pe≈6.
- Coarse-grid upstream weighting injects artificial dispersivity ≈ Δx/2 → smears fronts, **spreads mass outward and depresses peaks**.
- Park et al. (2021, *Adv. Water Resour.* 153) — numerical dispersion decreases with refinement and **corrupts calibration of physical dispersivity**.
- Benson et al. (2017, *Adv. Water Resour.* 99:15–37) — grid methods carry "masking effects of spurious mixing"; **a good fit on a coarse advective grid is not evidence of a correct model**. → Our DISV result (refinement *unmasks* a real discharge under-prediction the coarse grid's numerical dispersion fortuitously hid) is a documented phenomenon, not an anomaly.
- Scheme tradeoffs (Zheng & Wang 1999): upstream FD (dispersive), central (oscillatory), MOC/MMOC/HMOC, **TVD/ULTIMATE** (modern default; note MF6's TVD is more dispersive than MT3DMS's).

## C3. GW→SW discharge & streambed porewater (why discharge cells under-predict)
- **Sun, McMahon, Genereux et al. (2021, *Environ. Sci. Technol.* 55(2):793–803)** — Cape Fear: baseflow PFAS flux 32±7 kg/yr; cross-validated stream-water vs streambed-GW estimates (the validation approach).
- Jensen, Genereux et al. (2024, *Environ. Sci. Technol.* 58) — age-dated streambed GW + convolution for discharge concentration (forward grid models struggle there).
- **Lemke et al. (2024, *Sci. Total Environ.* 951:175672)** — PFAS across a GW-SW interface (House Street → Rogue, MI, **our site**): OM sorption + AWI retention complicate the interface.
- **Mojarrad et al. (2023, *Groundwater* 61(1):66–85)** — hyporheic flow fragments discharge into sub-metre "pinhole" stream tubes; **95% of upwelling areas <1.25 m²** → block-averaged cells dilute the peak discharge concentration by construction. (The root cause of discharge-cell under-prediction.)
- Tripathi et al. (2020/2021, *Groundwater Monit. Remediat.* 41(1)) — focused discharge over only 3–6% of reach.

## C4. Source-term & sorption (magnitude vs pattern)
- **Rafiei & Nejadhashemi (2023)** — trend captured, PFOA magnitude under-predicted (source/history limitation, not transport numerics).
- Rovero et al. (2021) / Adamson et al. (2022, PMC8788618) — Kd spans **3–5 log units**; fixed-Kd Freundlich SSL "inadequate to predict impacts to groundwater"; one input quartile → order-of-magnitude screening-level swing.
- Brusseau (2018), Guo et al. (2020), Brusseau & Guo (2021) — omitting AWI mis-states effective source loading + arrival; AWI-area uncertainty → 3–20 yr arrival range.
- USGS / ITRC framing: prescribed/screening-level models state purpose (pathway/extent/relative ranking, not point magnitude), declare source as the dominant uncertainty, and report order-of-magnitude/relative results — the framing our prescribed-source model can adopt.

**Bottom line for our paper:** every challenge we hit is documented and named in the literature —
order-of-magnitude is the accepted plume bar; coarse-grid numerical dispersion masking a real misfit is
a known phenomenon; discharge-cell under-prediction is the sub-grid "pinhole" problem; and prescribed-
source + Freundlich/AWI magnitude error is textbook. This lets us report the porewater bias as a
*characterized, literature-consistent* limitation rather than a defect.
