### 3.5 Calibration and validation design

Because in-stream PFOS transport is conditioned on the simulated water balance, calibration proceeds in two sequential stages—flow first, then PFAS parameters—so that hydrologic forcing is constrained before solute-transport parameters are fit, and PFAS-parameter error is not aliased onto flow error.

**Flow calibration.** Streamflow is calibrated against daily discharge at the U.S. Geological Survey gauges within and immediately downstream of HUC8 04100013, using the multi-memory particle-swarm optimizer (MMPSO) implemented on the SWATGenX platform, with single-population PSO retained as a fallback. Optimization uses an ensemble of approximately 24 particles [VERIFY final swarm size] over a Latin-hypercube-seeded initial design. Prior to optimization, a Morris elementary-effects screening (Morris 1991) ranks the candidate flow parameters—curve number, available water capacity, baseflow recession (alpha and the groundwater-delay term), lateral-flow travel time, and channel hydraulic conductivity and roughness, among others—so that non-influential parameters are fixed at default and only the sensitive subset enters the calibration vector; this is the recognized first-line control on equifinality (Beven & Binley 1992) and keeps the dimension of the search tractable despite the fine discretization. Goodness of fit is assessed with the Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency (NSE) and the Kling–Gupta efficiency (KGE) at daily and monthly aggregation, with percent bias (PBIAS) reported to constrain volumetric error. Predictive uncertainty is summarized by the P-factor (the fraction of observations bracketed by the 95% prediction band) and the R-factor (the mean band width relative to the observed standard deviation), following the SWAT-CUP convention (Abbaspour et al. 2015); a P-factor near or above the conventional 0.7 acceptability guideline with a contained R-factor is the target. A warm-up period precedes the evaluation window, and the record is partitioned into calibration and validation sub-periods so that performance is reported on data withheld from fitting.

**PFAS-parameter calibration.** With the water balance fixed, PFOS transport parameters are calibrated against Michigan EGLE and USEPA/WQP in-stream PFOS observations assigned to their hydrologically correct channels by the closest-drainage-area procedure of Section 3.4. The calibration does not treat soil concentrations as free scalars; instead it samples within the physically bounded ranges already emitted by the initialization—the land-use-conditioned initial soil PFOS bands (forest 0.005–0.01, agricultural/pasture 0.2–8, urban 0.2–30 µg/kg) and the national-source-overlay bands (EPA TRI/FRS, FAA Part 139, DoD AFFF sites, 100–4000 µg/kg)—so that adjustment remains within source-attributable, literature-consistent limits rather than drifting to arbitrary best-fit values. The Freundlich solid-phase capacity kf, mapped per layer from the six reference soils (S1–S6), is allowed to vary within ±30% of its assigned value to absorb local organic-carbon and texture mismatch while preserving the soil-class ordering; the Freundlich exponent n and the in-stream linear-Koc partitioning coefficient are treated as the additional adjustable transport parameters, again screened by Morris before fitting and grouped by class (land use, soil, source type) rather than assigned independently per reach, which holds the calibration-vector dimension nearly constant as reach count rises. Agreement between simulated and observed in-stream PFOS is evaluated at the assigned reaches using NSE/KGE on the concentration series where the observational density supports it, supplemented by paired observed-versus-simulated error statistics (PBIAS, RMSE) where records are sparse, with the same P-factor/R-factor uncertainty reporting.

**Dynamic validation.** Beyond static fit, the calibrated model is confronted with the watershed's post-source-control trajectory: the ~99% water-column PFOS decline following upstream control, and the contrasting fish-tissue plateau attributed to sediment legacy (Endicott et al. 2025). Reproducing the water-column recovery while remaining consistent with a persistent sediment-bound reservoir is a demanding test of the model's water–sediment partitioning that fitting a single concentration series could not provide. Throughout, equifinality is treated as managed—through Morris screening, class-based parameter grouping, and explicit uncertainty bands—rather than eliminated.
