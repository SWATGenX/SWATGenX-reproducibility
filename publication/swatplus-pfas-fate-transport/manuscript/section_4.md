## 3.3 Soil-PFAS initialization

The soil compartment is the principal reservoir and long-term source term for PFOS in this system, so its initialization governs both the magnitude and the spatial structure of simulated loading. We initialize two distinct classes of quantity at the resolution of each HRU soil layer: (i) the sorption parameters that control three-phase partitioning—the Freundlich capacity coefficient *k*_f, the Freundlich exponent *n*, and the median grain diameter *d*₅₀—which are derived deterministically from soil properties already carried in the model; and (ii) the initial solid-phase PFOS concentration, which is assigned as a literature-anchored *range* by land use and source proximity and then handed to calibration. We state at the outset which steps are automated from national data and which are anchored to literature values, because the two carry different epistemic weight: the sorption parameters are physically constrained by measured soil texture and organic carbon, whereas the initial concentrations are bounded estimates that calibration is expected to refine within their assigned envelopes.

### Sorption parameters from gSSURGO

PFOS solid–aqueous partitioning is represented by a Freundlich isotherm, *C*_s = *k*_f · *C*_w^*n*, consistent with the source model (Rafiei & Nejadhashemi, 2023). The pair (*k*_f, *n*) and the grain diameter *d*₅₀ are assigned by analogy matching: for each HRU soil layer we extract organic-carbon content and texture (clay/sand fractions) from gSSURGO—properties the SWATGenX pipeline already ingests nationally for every layer—and classify the layer to whichever of six reference soils, S1–S6, most closely matches in organic carbon and texture by nearest-neighbor distance. The reference soils and their isotherm parameters are taken from the controlled PFOS sorption measurements of Li et al. (2019), spanning *k*_f ≈ 126–450, *n* ≈ 0.33–0.53, and *d*₅₀ ≈ 0.04–0.10 mm across the set [VERIFY the per-soil S1–S6 (*k*_f, *n*, *d*₅₀) values against Li et al. (2019), STOTEN 649:504–514, Table; the numeric table was not reproduced in the source-model supplement and must be transcribed from the primary]. Because organic carbon is the dominant control on PFOS solid-phase affinity, this match propagates the spatial texture and organic-carbon heterogeneity already resolved by gSSURGO into a spatially varying sorption field at no additional data cost. This is the most fully automated element of the initialization: it requires no PFAS-specific input layer, only the soil survey that underlies every SWATGenX model. The literature anchor enters once, as the six-soil reference set; everything downstream is deterministic. In calibration the *k*_f field is allowed to vary within a bounded multiplier of its analogy-derived value (see Section 3.5), preserving the gSSURGO-imposed spatial pattern while admitting a global scaling adjustment.

### Initial solid-phase PFOS by land use and source overlay

Initial PFOS concentrations cannot be drawn from a queryable national measured-soil layer, because none exists at the spatial completeness this model requires; we therefore adopt the tiered, range-based scheme of the source model (Rafiei & Nejadhashemi, 2023), generalized to national data sources. Every HRU first receives a land-use background range, classified from NLCD/CDL: forest and natural cover 0.005–0.01 µg/kg, agricultural and pasture 0.2–8 µg/kg, and developed/urban 0.2–30 µg/kg. These bounds reflect the order-of-magnitude separation between remote and developed soils reported in large soil-PFAS compilations (e.g., Brusseau et al., 2020) and are consistent with the Table-1 ranges of the source model.

Onto this background we apply additive national source overlays for known and presumptive contaminated sites, identified by spatial intersection rather than distance decay—gradients in the simulated field are allowed to emerge from transport rather than being imposed at initialization. HRUs intersecting PFAS-handling facilities from the U.S. EPA Toxics Release Inventory and Facility Registry Service (TRI/FRS), FAA Part 139–certificated airports (a proxy for aqueous film-forming foam, AFFF, fire-training activity), or Department of Defense AFFF installations are assigned an elevated source-zone range of 100–4,000 µg/kg, with the elevated concentration confined to a clamped source-pad footprint rather than the full facility buffer. This reproduces the source model's contaminated-site treatment while substituting national, programmatically queryable federal layers (TRI/FRS via EPA Envirofacts, the FAA Part 139 list, and DoD installation inventories) for the state-specific facility data of the original Huron application. Where state data are richer than the federal layers—as for Michigan—they may supplement, but are not required by, the national pipeline.

All concentrations are emitted as *ranges*, not point values, and these ranges constitute prior bounds handed to calibration. We make this explicit because it bears directly on the claim discipline of the work: the initialization fixes the spatial *structure* of the source field (which HRUs are background, biosolids-influenced, or source-zone, and how sorption varies with soil) from data, while leaving the operative *magnitudes* within each class to be identified by the optimizer. This is a managed treatment of equifinality, not an elimination of it; the ranges are wide by design, and the burden of constraining them falls on calibration against in-stream observations.

## 3.4 Observation-to-channel assignment

Testing simulated PFOS against ambient observations requires that each monitoring record be associated with the channel reach it actually represents. In a coarsely discretized model this association is ambiguous—an observation may fall within an aggregated subbasin that lumps a contaminated tributary with unaffected drainage—and the resulting representativeness error is conflated with model error during calibration. The fine network resolved here (3,119 channels) reduces that ambiguity by providing reach units close to the scale of the observations themselves.

Ambient PFAS surface-water stations are drawn from the Michigan EGLE surface-water monitoring program and the USEPA Water Quality Portal (WQP). Each station is snapped to the nearest SWAT+ channel reach by planar distance in the CONUS Albers equal-area projection (EPSG:5070), with the candidate reach selected to be consistent in drainage area—the assignment is closest-drainage-area-aware rather than purely geometric, so a station is matched to a reach of comparable contributing area rather than to a physically nearer but hydrologically inconsistent channel. The drainage-area criterion is motivated conceptually by drainage-area-ratio (DAR) reasoning, under which streamflow and load scale approximately with contributing area; we use DAR as a conceptual scale for representativeness, not as a numerically transferable validity band. On a test sub-watershed, 58 EGLE stations were assigned by this procedure with a median snap distance of 3.9 m, indicating that at this network resolution the great majority of stations fall essentially on a resolved channel rather than requiring substantial displacement; the full HUC8 station set is larger, and its assignment statistics are reported with the results. The reach-resolved network and this assignment procedure together establish the spatial correspondence on which the in-stream calibration of Section 3.5 depends.
