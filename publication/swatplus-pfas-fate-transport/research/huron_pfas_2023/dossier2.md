# DOSSIER — Huron River Watershed PFAS: Sources & Enforcement, 2023–2026 (Stream 2)

Scope: Tribar Technologies (Wixom), Adept Plastic Finishing, Wixom WWTP, the City of Wixom industrial pretreatment program, biosolids land-application restrictions, and identified dischargers — events local to the Huron River watershed since the 2023 Water Research paper. Confirmed events are distinguished from proposals. All numbers and dates are sourced inline.

## Bottom line up front
- The single dominant PFAS source identified pre-2023 — Tribar's PFOS discharge to the City of Wixom sanitary sewer (which passes through the Wixom WWTP to Norton Creek → Huron River, ~5 mi upstream of Kent Lake) — was substantially controlled by the 2018 EGLE-ordered filtration. Officials now describe a >90–95% (later cited as ~99%) reduction in river PFOS vs. 2018 baseline. This is the major confirmed improvement since the paper. (HRWC's Daniel Brown, WEMU, July 2025.)
- BUT two new/continuing enforcement vectors emerged in 2025: (1) an **April 2025 federal criminal conviction** of Tribar (Clean Water Act, $200,000 fine + probation) for the 2022 hexavalent-chromium spill; and (2) a **June 12–13, 2025 Michigan AG civil lawsuit** against Tribar AND Adept Plastic Finishing alleging *continuing PFOS-laden stormwater* discharge from the now-closed Wixom sites, with one outfall measured as high as **220,000 ppt PFOS**.
- The Wixom facilities **closed (~April 2024–April 2025; sources vary), operations moved to Howell** — but contamination on the properties persists, which is the crux of the 2025 civil suit.
- A parallel, watershed-relevant track: **biosolids**. Wixom WWTP biosolids were PFOS-contaminated (effluent ~269 ppt PFOS; biosolids ~2,150 ppb PFOS) and were land-applied to ~6 farm fields; EGLE's investigation of the "Wixom WWTP Biosolids Fields Area of Interest" (Livingston County) continued through a June 2023 addendum, and Michigan's statewide biosolids land-application restrictions tightened effective Jan 1, 2024.

## DATED TIMELINE (2023 → 2026)

**2018 (baseline, pre-paper context):** EGLE ordered Tribar to submit a PFOS-reduction work plan (due Oct 19, 2018) and install filtration after PFAS was found in Huron River fish; the City of Wixom WWTP effluent had exceeded the Rule 57 water-quality value (11 ppt PFOS). MDHHS issued the "Do Not Eat" fish advisory on the main stem below Norton Creek near Wixom. Filtration produced what officials call a national "success story." [Confirmed; context]

**June 2023:** EGLE published an **addendum** to "Evaluation of Wixom WWTP Biosolids Land Application Sites," summarizing 2020–2022 investigations of the six farm fields that received industrially-impacted Wixom biosolids (soil, surface water, groundwater, plant tissue, manure, beef, residential wells). A March 2021 shallow monitoring well at a Livingston County farm had exceeded the state groundwater PFOA criterion; residential wells nearby tested below drinking-water standards. [Confirmed]

**Aug 2023:** Michigan released the 2023 Eat Safe Fish Guides (Huron River advisory maintained). [Confirmed]

**Jan 1, 2024:** Michigan's **biosolids land-application interim strategy** (with 2024 modifications) took effect: reduced application rate of **1.5 dry tons/acre** (or an EGLE-approved alternative risk-mitigation strategy) for impacted biosolids; biosolids with PFOS+PFOA below **20 µg/kg** may be applied without added requirements. Directly relevant to Huron-area farms historically receiving Wixom biosolids. [Confirmed — regulatory]

**~April 2024 (one source) / weeks before April 2025 sentencing (another source):** Tribar **sold**; purchaser **shut the Wixom facilities** and moved Tribar Technologies operations to **Howell**. (Note: sources disagree on exact closure date — Bridge/CBS-adjacent reporting says April 2024; Michigan Public ties closure to "weeks before" the April 2025 federal sentencing. Treat closure as 2024–2025, the move to Howell as confirmed.) [Confirmed event, date uncertain]

**April 29, 2025:** **Tribar Technologies pleaded guilty / was sentenced** in federal court (E.D. Mich.) for criminal discharge in violation of the **federal Clean Water Act** for the 2022 hexavalent-chromium release. **$200,000 fine + federal probation.** The 2022 incident: ~15,000 gal of untreated hexavalent-chromium wastewater placed in a holding tank July 23, 2022; ~10,000 gal discharged the evening of July 29, 2022 to the Wixom sewer; an employee disabled numerous (reported ~460) warning alarms. Tribar has since added automated shut-off valves, remote monitoring, expanded WWTP staff, and an Environmental Management System. (This is the chromium case, not PFAS — but it's the enforcement hammer on the same actor/same outfall path.) [Confirmed]

**June 12–13, 2025:** Michigan **AG Dana Nessel filed a civil complaint** against **Tribar Technologies Inc. and Adept Plastic Finishing Inc.** (the sister facility) alleging multiple violations of Michigan's Natural Resources and Environmental Protection Act — including that, **despite closure of the Wixom facilities, PFOS-laden stormwater continues to discharge** from both sites. **One Tribar outfall measured as high as 220,000 ppt PFOS.** Tribar had received **four state violation notices since 2018.** The suit seeks **civil penalties + contamination cleanup**, and invokes the Michigan Business Corporation Act to challenge Tribar's certificate of authority to operate in Michigan. The complaint also re-pleads the 2022 chromium discharge through the Wixom WWTP. [Confirmed filing — allegations not yet adjudicated]

**2025 Eat Safe Fish Guide / MDHHS guideline change:** Michigan lowered its "Do Not Eat" PFOS-in-fish threshold from **300 ppb to 50 ppb**, expanding statewide Do-Not-Eat waterbodies from **92 to 188**. The Huron River advisory persists; HRWC's Daniel Brown says the advisory may persist "indefinitely" for some species, with possible partial lifting for some fish as populations recover. [Confirmed]

**Feb 2026:** EGLE Fish Contaminant Monitoring Program has collected/scheduled Huron River fillet samples for PFAS analysis (ongoing monitoring). [Confirmed — ongoing]

**2025 (status: PROPOSED):** A Michigan legislative **PFAS bill package** was introduced to strengthen the state's PFAS response (HRWC covered it). Not enacted as of this research; treat as proposal. [Proposal]

## SOURCE/ACTOR DETAIL

**Tribar Technologies (Wixom) — the primary identified PFAS source**
- Pre-2023: PFOS to City of Wixom sanitary sewer → Wixom WWTP → Norton Creek → Huron River. 2018 filtration drove river PFOS down >90–95% (later ~99% vs 2018). [Confirmed]
- 2022 chromium spill → **April 2025 federal CWA conviction, $200k + probation.** [Confirmed]
- Wixom plant **closed**, operations to **Howell**. [Confirmed]
- **June 2025 civil suit** for continuing PFOS stormwater (up to **220,000 ppt** at one outfall). [Confirmed filing]

**Adept Plastic Finishing Inc. (Wixom)** — newly named co-defendant in the June 2025 civil suit; same continuing-PFOS-stormwater allegation. (This is a 2025 addition vs. the 2023-paper era, when Tribar was the named focus.) [Confirmed filing]

**City of Wixom WWTP & IPP** — discharge point for both the chromium spill and historic PFOS. Under EGLE's **IPP PFAS Initiative** (launched early 2018, 95 municipal WWTPs statewide), Wixom worked with Tribar to cut sewer PFOS to meet the Rule 57 value (11 ppt) in Norton Creek. Documented effluent ~**269 ppt PFOS**; biosolids ~**2,150 ppb PFOS**. [Confirmed]

**Biosolids / Huron-area farms** — Wixom WWTP Biosolids Fields Area of Interest (Livingston County): ~6 fields tested across multiple media; 2021 groundwater PFOA exceedance at one farm; June 2023 addendum; statewide land-application limits tightened Jan 1, 2024 (1.5 dt/acre; 20 µg/kg threshold). [Confirmed]
