# STREAM 4 — PFAS REMEDIATION & WATERSHED MANAGEMENT, HURON RIVER WATERSHED (2023–2026)

Scope: remediation/treatment/source-reduction implemented and its measured effect; HRWC activities; state/federal funding; management decisions (fish/foam advisories driving action); outcomes. CONFIRMED events flagged vs. PROPOSALS. Note: much of the *physical* source-reduction (activated-carbon treatment at the Wixom WWTP / Tribar) predates 2023; the 2023–2026 story is dominated by enforcement, advisory tightening, and policy/funding — and a notable *backsliding* event (continuing stormwater discharge from the shuttered Tribar site).

## DATED TIMELINE

- **2018 (baseline, pre-period):** EGLE/Ann Arbor/Wixom identify elevated PFOS in Huron River fish and surface water. Source traced to Tribar Plant 4 (chrome plater) → Wixom sanitary sewer → Wixom WWTP → Norton Creek → Huron River. Sewer PFOS measured at **28,000 ppt**. Statewide-first "DO NOT EAT THE FOAM" + Do Not Eat Fish advisory issued. EGLE orders activated-carbon filtration at the source (Oct 2018). [This is the remediation that drives the later measured decline.]

- **Oct 26, 2020 (measured effect of treatment):** EGLE reports a **99.8% decline** in PFAS downstream of Norton Creek over ~2.5 years; max detection downstream of Norton Creek dropped from **1,400 ppt (July 2018) to 6.1 ppt (Aug 2020)**. EGLE explicitly attributes the drop to the City of Wixom working with the upstream industrial facility (Tribar) to install PFOS treatment, dramatically cutting WWTP discharge to Norton Creek. CONFIRMED. (This is the single best "remediation → measured outcome" data point; the cleanup mechanism is source treatment, not in-river remediation.)

- **Aug 2022 (setback / driver):** Tribar releases **10,000 gallons of hexavalent-chromium wastewater** through the Wixom WWTP to the Huron River, triggering a multi-county "do not contact" advisory into Washtenaw County. This event re-energized enforcement and HRWC advocacy. CONFIRMED.

- **Nov 1, 2022:** EGLE issues a new wastewater permit to the City of Wixom. HRWC publicly criticized it ("State Permit Fails to Protect the Huron River") as doing "little more than the bare minimum," still allowing Tribar to discharge PFAS through the WWTP, upstream of a drinking-water source. **Compliance deadline: 2025.** CONFIRMED (permit), HRWC position is advocacy.

- **2024 (advisory science tightening — management decision):** MDHHS updates the Eat Safe Fish program. Michigan's **"Do Not Eat" PFOS trigger lowered from 300 ppb to 50 ppb** in fish tissue (emerging science: PFOS more toxic than thought). Effect on Huron watershed: the advisory area expanded/strengthened rather than relaxed. CONFIRMED.

- **2024 (foam — management gap):** Reports note that despite known PFAS foam in SE Michigan (Huron) and other Michigan waters, public beaches still lacked posted "avoid the foam" warning signage; community advocates (Ecology Center Healthy Stuff Lab) pushed for permanent signage. Foam advisories for the Huron (issued 2018–2020) remain in effect. CONFIRMED (advisory persists); permanent signage = PROPOSAL/recommendation, not implemented.

- **Feb 2025:** Tribar plea agreement (federal/state) over the 2022 chromium spill. CONFIRMED (Tribar Plea Agreement document).

- **Apr 2025:** Tribar **criminally charged, fined $200,000, and placed on 2-year probation** for the 2022 incident. CONFIRMED.

- **June 6, 2025:** "Polluter Pay" six-bill package re-introduced in the Michigan Legislature (stricter cleanup standards, transparency, polluter-funded cleanup). HRWC actively backs it, citing **>1,000 contaminated sites in the Huron watershed**, including Tribar PFAS and the Gelman 1,4-dioxane plume. CONFIRMED (bills introduced); passage = PENDING/PROPOSAL.

- **June 12–16, 2025 (key enforcement event of the period):** Michigan AG **Dana Nessel files a civil complaint** against **Tribar Technologies Inc. and Adept Plastic Finishing Inc.** Core allegation: even though the Wixom facilities have **closed and operations relocated to Howell**, the properties **continue to discharge PFAS-contaminated stormwater** into the Huron River, violating state environmental law and a prior EGLE consent order. One **stormwater outfall tested as high as 220,000 ppt PFAS**. The suit covers past/ongoing illegal discharges and past unlawful air emissions across five Wixom facilities. **Remedies sought:** force cleanup of the contamination, upgrade stormwater infrastructure, long-term monitoring, civil penalties, and a challenge to Tribar's certificate of authority (Michigan Business Corporation Act) — i.e., potentially barring it from operating in Michigan. CONFIRMED filing; outcome PENDING. Significance for Stream 4: the *primary historical source is still leaking via stormwater from the abandoned site* — source reduction at the sewer/WWTP succeeded, but the site itself is not remediated.

- **Feb–July 2025 (federal-standard risk):** Reporting flags that weakened federal PFAS drinking-water standards under the Trump administration could undercut protections relevant to the Huron, which is an upstream drinking-water source. Context/risk, not a watershed action.

- **Nov 12, 2025:** HRWC hosts its **State of the Huron Conference** (part of HRWC's 60-year anniversary) with PFAS, stormwater runoff, and shrinking natural areas as headline topics. CONFIRMED. HRWC's role remains monitoring + advocacy (no regulatory authority).

## KEY FINDINGS FOR STREAM 4

1. **The remediation that worked was source treatment, not in-river/watershed remediation.** Activated-carbon PFOS treatment installed 2018 at the Tribar source upstream of the Wixom WWTP. Measured effect: **~99–99.8% reduction** in Huron PFAS downstream of Norton Creek (1,400 → ~6 ppt). No evidence of in-stream/sediment PFAS remediation; sediment is instead being *studied* as a continuing reservoir (Kent Lake/Proud Lake fish-sediment-macroinvertebrate sampling).

2. **Source reduction is incomplete — the shuttered Tribar site still discharges.** As of June 2025 the AG alleges ongoing PFAS stormwater discharge with an outfall up to **220,000 ppt**. This is the most important 2023–2026 development for a fate-and-transport model: the dominant point source is reduced but not eliminated, and now enters partly via uncontrolled stormwater rather than the WWTP.

3. **Management is shifting from monitoring to enforcement + tighter advisory science.** Drivers: AG civil suit (June 2025), criminal conviction/$200k fine (Apr 2025), MDHHS lowering the PFOS Do-Not-Eat trigger 300→50 ppb (2024). Fish advisory persists (upriver of Ypsilanti for the foreseeable future; relaxed below I-275); foam advisory persists; permanent foam signage still only a recommendation.

4. **Dedicated Huron-specific BIL/IIJA or MI-PFAS-fund line items: not confirmed.** Michigan PFAS/clean-water funding (>$4B since 2019; MI Clean Water grants $9.7M/$23.3M/$27.1M tranches in 2024; C2R2 and EPA Emerging Contaminants in Small/Disadvantaged Communities grants; ~$833k FY2025 BIL EC reallotment) exists statewide, but searches surfaced **no confirmed grant earmarked to a Huron-watershed PFAS project**. HRWC's strategy is to make the polluter pay (Polluter Pay bills, AG suit) rather than fund cleanup with public money. Treat any "Huron got BIL money for PFAS" claim as UNCONFIRMED.

5. **HRWC (2023–2026) activities:** PFAS monitoring/data aggregation (hrwc.org/PFAS), public comment on the Wixom permit, advocacy for the 2025 Polluter Pay package and for PFAS-in-consumer-goods/biosolids legislation, and the Nov 12 2025 State of the Huron conference. HRWC has no regulatory authority; its lever is advocacy + relationships with legislators.
