# HURON RIVER WATERSHED — PFAS DEVELOPMENTS SINCE 2023
## Stream 3: Monitoring & New Science (2023–2026)

Scope: Huron-local surface-water, fish, and sediment monitoring + new science published 2023–2026. The headline finding for a SWAT+ fate-and-transport rebuild: **source control worked at the WWTP/water-column level, but PFOS in fish has PLATEAUED ABOVE the advisory threshold — and 2025 science attributes this to in-lake SEDIMENT acting as a secondary internal source.** This is the single most modeling-relevant change since the 2023 Water Research paper.

## DATED TIMELINE

**Feb 2023 — Community fish-testing study (CONFIRMED, published Mar 2023).** Ecology Center Healthy Stuff Lab + HRWC + Friends of the Rouge; anglers hand-caught **100 fish, 12 species, 15 locations** across the Huron and Rouge rivers, analyzed for 40 PFAS.
- At least one of 14 PFAS in **every fish**; total PFAS **11–133 ppb**.
- PFOS dominant: **58% of PFAS content in Huron fish** (46% Rouge).
- Michigan regulates only 2 of the 14 detected compounds.
- Huron species sampled: walleye, carp, catfish, pumpkinseed, black crappie.

**May 2024 — Novel-PFAS Huron-Erie Corridor study (CONFIRMED).** *Science of the Total Environment*, article S0048969724034727. 27 sites in the Lake Huron→Lake Erie corridor (includes Rouge AOC), 92 PFAS via UHPLC-MS/MS.
- PFOS and PFOA detected at **all sites and in all matrices**, often above EPA interim health advisories.
- Surface water dominated by **PFHxA** (above prior Great Lakes averages) — a shift away from the legacy PFOS-only narrative.
- Sediment dominated by **PFOS**, plus a novel congener **FPePA (3-perfluoropentyl propanoic acid)** quantified in sediment.

**Apr 2024 — EPA national drinking-water MCLs finalized (national, but hits Huron directly).** 4 ppt for PFOA and PFOS. Relevant because the Huron supplies ~85% of Ann Arbor's drinking water; Ann Arbor runs advanced (GAC) filtration. Michigan's own MCLs remain 8 ppt PFOA / 16 ppt PFOS.

**Feb 26, 2025 — Federal rollback raises Huron risk (CONFIRMED rollback of a PROPOSAL).** Trump EPA withdrew the Biden-era proposed Clean Water Act effluent limitation guidelines (ELGs) for PFAS dischargers (chemical manufacturers). HRWC framed it as shifting the burden to states. The April 2024 drinking-water MCLs remained in effect as of this report; the *discharge-limit* proposal was killed before finalization.

**30 May 2025 — USGS Great Lakes tributaries baseline (CONFIRMED).** Loken, Corsi, Alvarez et al., *Environmental Toxicology and Chemistry* 44(6):1503. USGS Upper Midwest WSC + EPA. 62 tributary sites / 20 watersheds. **Note: Huron River itself NOT sampled** (Au Sable in the Lake Huron drainage was). Useful as a regional reference / watershed-attribute model:
- Surface water median **PFOS 4.6 ng/L, PFOA 5.9 ng/L**.
- Sediment median **PFOS 132 ng/kg, max 20,800 ng/kg**.
- Urban land cover, airports/catchment, and WWTP flow fraction significantly (p<0.05) drive PFAS — directly relevant to SWAT+ source parameterization.

**Jun 3, 2025 — MDHHS tightens fish "Do Not Eat" threshold (CONFIRMED).** Eat Safe Fish guide lowered the PFOS Do-Not-Eat trigger **from 300 ppb to 50 ppb** (6-fold). Statewide Do-Not-Eat waterbodies rose **92 → 188**. New/expanded Huron-watershed guidance: **Whitmore Lake, Bruin Lake, Portage Creek (Huron R. to Hi-Land Lake Dam).** A Do-Not-Eat advisory remains on the Huron mainstem. (Source thresholds are a tissue benchmark, not new monitoring — but they re-flag the watershed.)

**Jul 1, 2025 — Kent Lake sediment-source study (CONFIRMED) — the key new science.** Endicott, Silva-Wilkinson, McCauley, Armstrong, *Integrated Environmental Assessment and Management* (IEAM). Kent Lake (Huron impoundment, 5 mi downstream of Wixom WWTP/Norton Creek) vs reference lake.
- **PFOS decline in fish tissue reached a PLATEAU at a level still EXCEEDING the Do-Not-Eat threshold**, despite the upstream WWTP/Tribar source control.
- Evidence "consistent with PFAS in sediment acting as an ongoing source" to Kent Lake biota — especially **PFOS and 6:2 FTS**.
- Companion sediment/food-web work (PubMed 39903053) collecting fish, sediment, macroinvertebrates from **Kent Lake and Proud Lake** to test the sediment-reservoir hypothesis.

## TREND: ARE CONCENTRATIONS DROPPING AFTER SOURCE CONTROL?

Split answer — this is the nuance to carry into the SWAT+ model:

- **Water column / source: YES, dramatically — but this is mostly PRE-2023.** Wixom worked with Tribar Technologies to install PFOS treatment upstream of the WWTP. Norton Creek fell **5,600 ppt (2018) → 12.2 ppt (Aug 2020), a 99.8% drop**; Huron downstream of Norton Creek fell **1,400 ppt → 6.1 ppt** by Aug 2020. EGLE's "PFAS dropping" messaging (WHMI Oct 26, 2020) predates the 2023 paper and is not a new post-2023 result.
- **Fish tissue: NO — plateaued above the advisory threshold.** The 2025 Kent Lake study is the post-2023 development: water-column control did not translate into fish dropping below safe-to-eat levels, and **sediment is now the implicated internal source.** This is why MDHHS *tightened* (not relaxed) the fish threshold in June 2025 and the Do-Not-Eat advisory persists.

For comparison to the 2018–2021 data the Water Research paper used (Norton Creek max 5,600 ppt; Huron max 1,400 ppt PFOS): current water-column maxima are single-digit ppt, but the **mass has partitioned into sediment**, and fish PFOS is the binding constraint, not water concentration.

## MODELING IMPLICATIONS FOR THE SWAT+ PFAS REBUILD
1. **Add a sediment compartment / sediment-pore-water exchange** — the 2025 Kent Lake finding makes sediment a first-class state variable, not a sink. A water-column-only port will mis-predict the fish-tissue plateau.
2. **Move beyond PFOS-only.** Post-2023 science shows PFHxA (water) and FPePA / 6:2 FTS (sediment) as significant; the 2023 paper's PFOS focus is now incomplete.
3. **Calibration target shift:** the relevant constraint is now fish-tissue PFOS vs the 50 ppb threshold, not water-column ppt.
4. **Source term is largely shut off** (Tribar/Wixom controlled pre-2023), so post-2018 dynamics are dominated by legacy storage release — a transient/desorption regime, not steady loading.
