\section{Hydraulic-ingredient coverage}
Measured conductivity is never in a driller's log; it must be derived from a pump test (specific capacity
$\rightarrow$ transmissivity) and a screened thickness. Table~\ref{tab:hydraulics} audits, per ingested
state, which raw ingredients exist and how populated they are (cell $=$ max non-null \% in a 1{,}500-row
sample of any matching field; name-based lower bound). Static water level and total depth form a broad
backbone; the pump-test fields are scarce.

\noindent\emph{Reading it:} direct specific capacity appears in only NV and WI; drawdown in AZ, NV, WI;
measured transmissivity in NV alone. A national derived-conductivity layer is therefore feasible only where
yield and drawdown co-occur; elsewhere conductivity must fall back to lithology-facies estimates anchored to
the observed log. Full matrix and generator: \code{hydraulic\_coverage.md} and
\code{audit\_hydraulic\_coverage.py} (under \code{national\_gw\_inventory/}).

\section{Extraction schema and validation}
The forced JSON schema returns, per log: ordered intervals (top/bottom feet, verbatim description,
normalized class), total depth, static water level, a legibility flag, and a self-confidence score.
Validation across the seven calibrated states found faithful transcription of driller shorthand
(including preserved misspellings), legibility flags on illegible scans, and \emph{empty} arrays rather
than fabricated intervals on blank logs --- the key trust signal. A quantitative ground-truth comparison
against a state agency's own hand-transcriptions ($n=1{,}499$ wells) is reported in the main text.

\section{Data sources and access provenance}
\label{sec:provenance}
This section documents, for every state we touched, exactly how its well and lithology data were
accessed: the custodian agency and dataset, the access endpoint (a bulk-file portal or, more often, an
ArcGIS/REST feature service or a per-well report endpoint), the access method, the harvested point count,
the route by which lithology is recovered, and the date the endpoint was last accessed and verified
(2026-06-24 throughout). Table~\ref{tab:provenance} is the citable per-state ledger; each agency portal
also carries a data-citation entry in the bibliography (citekeys \code{data\_<st>\_<agency>}).

The lithology route falls into three provenance \emph{tiers}, ordered by extraction cost.
\textbf{(a) Structured / bulk} (no vision model): lithology arrives already machine-readable --- a bulk
file (CA OSWCR CSVs, MN CWI GeoPackage, MO/MSDIS shapefile), an inline interval table or wide columns in
the REST record (MI, MT, AL, KY, IL-bulk), or a server-rendered HTML grid scraped per well (OK, IA, VT,
NM). \textbf{(b) Born-digital text-layer PDF} (cheap text-only LLM): the per-well report is a digital PDF
whose strata table survives \code{pdftotext}, so no image tokens are needed (TX, OH, NY, IL via the
\code{water\_summary} endpoint, MA). \textbf{(c) Scanned PDF} (vision-language model): the log is a raster
scan of a paper form and must be read with a vision model (WA, MS, NE, UT, ID, OR, WI, FL, NV, SD, KS).
For ten states the data is present but was, at audit time, \emph{out of reach} of an automated anonymous
client, and we record \emph{why}, because the obstruction is procedural rather than an absence of data:
TN, NH, and AZ return HTTP~403 to our primary datacenter IP (an AWS~ELB/WAF, an Akamai edge rule, and a
Cloudflare JS/Turnstile challenge, respectively). These are IP-reputation blocks, not data restrictions,
and are only partially circumventable: re-issuing the requests from a fresh AWS~EC2 elastic IP cleared
\emph{TN} --- whose report page in fact renders a \emph{structured HTML} lithology table requiring no vision
model, from which we then harvested 77{,}836 wells --- whereas the Akamai (NH) and Cloudflare (AZ) rules
fingerprint datacenter IP ranges generally and still returned 403, so those two require a residential or
headless-browser egress. WY places
its statements of completion behind an eGov login with no anonymous handler; PA's scanned records exist
only as GIF images keyed by an internal identifier with no public record-to-image mapping; and VA, ME, and
SC publish only located-well tables, with the actual completion reports held in internal storage or
released by request. None of these is a confidentiality restriction --- the records are public; they are
simply not yet harvestable by an automated, anonymous client.
