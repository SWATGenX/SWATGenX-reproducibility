\section{Introduction}

High-resolution national hydrography and environmental datasets make detailed SWAT\textsuperscript{+} model construction possible across broad spatial extents, but reproducible model assembly remains difficult because routing topology, attributes, and export structure can vary across analysts and study sites.
National SWAT-family platforms such as HAWQS \citep{Yen2016HAWQS} and BASINS \citep{EPABASINSFramework} emphasize decision support and analyst-driven setup on medium-resolution hydrography rather than automated, auditable assembly from NHDPlus High Resolution (NHDPlus HR) at national scale.
This paper evaluates SWATGenX as a reference implementation of an automated NHDPlus HR workflow that converts U.S.\ national vectors and ancillary layers into SWAT\textsuperscript{+}-compatible project structures with explicit preprocessing rules and quality-assurance checks.

\subsection{Contribution and scope}

The narrow contribution is auditable, automated conversion of NHDPlus HR and national ancillary data into structurally consistent, executable SWAT\textsuperscript{+} projects, with documented preprocessing trade-offs and evidence that those projects can enter standard downstream calibration and sensitivity workflows.
The evaluation is \emph{not} a national hydrologic validation study: reported streamflow metrics on two gage basins are workflow diagnostics only.

\subsection{Evidence tiers}

Evidence is organized in three tiers (Table~\ref{tab:objective-evidence-map}).
\textbf{Primary} evidence (Objectives~1--2) documents implementation auditability and quantified hydrography preprocessing outcomes, including a drainage-area fidelity audit at gaged channels.
\textbf{Secondary} evidence (Objectives~3--4) reports structural scaling, model-assembly cost, and downstream workflow readiness on selected domains.
\textbf{Supporting} evidence (Objective~5) reports measured simulation cost on one documented host/build configuration.

\subsection{Evaluation model sets}

Table~\ref{tab:model-roster} lists the eight catalog Model IDs used in this evaluation.
Three size classes (S/M/L) benchmark structural products and primary runtime; three scaling cases (X20/X40/X60) extend the runtime ladder; two controlled gage basins test calibration and Morris sensitivity workflows.
The matrix spans humid subtropical, semi-arid, and snow-influenced temperate domains in the eastern and western U.S.\ but does not represent arid ephemeral systems, heavily regulated reservoir complexes, dense urban cores, or coastal tidal networks.
A CONUS-capable workflow is demonstrated on these selected domains only.

\subsection{Research objectives}

Objectives are grouped by evidence tier rather than presented as five equal-weight claims.

\textbf{Primary.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Implementation and workflow architecture.} Can national high-resolution hydrography and environmental datasets be converted into structurally consistent, executable, and auditable SWAT\textsuperscript{+} projects through a documented automated workflow?
  \item \textbf{Hydrography preprocessing outcomes.} Are preprocessing rules stated explicitly, are quality-assurance outcomes quantified relative to original NHDPlus HR sources, and does the executable model preserve cumulative drainage area at gaged channels?
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Secondary.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \setcounter{enumi}{2}
  \item \textbf{Structural scalability and model-assembly cost.} How do HRU, channel, and subbasin counts and assembly wall time scale across the S/M/L benchmark size classes?
  \item \textbf{Downstream workflow readiness.} Can generated projects be initialized, calibrated, verified on independent holdout periods, and analyzed with Morris sensitivity workflows on controlled gage basins?
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Supporting.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \setcounter{enumi}{4}
  \item \textbf{Simulation runtime benchmark.} What is measured SWAT\textsuperscript{+} simulation cost on one documented host/build configuration for the published benchmark domains?
\end{enumerate}
