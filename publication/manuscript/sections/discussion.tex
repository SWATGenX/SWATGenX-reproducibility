\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Principal finding}

National NHDPlus HR and ancillary U.S.\ datasets can be converted automatically into structurally consistent, executable, and auditable SWAT\textsuperscript{+} projects through the documented SWATGenX workflow.
Primary evidence comprises a layered implementation with inspectable outputs and a stated preprocessing rule set with quantified structural changes relative to original NHDPlus HR sources (Objectives~1--2).
Secondary evidence comprises structural and assembly-cost scaling across three benchmark size classes and successful execution of initialization, split-sample calibration and verification, and Morris sensitivity on two controlled gage basins (Objectives~3--4).
Supporting evidence comprises predictable simulation cost on one documented host/build configuration (Objective~5).
That evidence supports reproducible national preprocessing and project generation; it does \emph{not} establish nationwide hydrologic validity, cross-tool superiority, or readiness for regulatory filing without expert review.

\subsection{Objective-by-objective synthesis}

Objective 1 (implementation structure). Figures~\ref{fig:workflow} and \ref{fig:architecture-layers} show that generation follows a layered, auditable design; manifest classes in Table~\ref{tab:package-manifest-abridged} confirm standard vector layers, climate station files, SQLite project tables, and QA sidecars rather than ad hoc folder layouts per analyst.

Objective 2 (preprocessing rules). The NHDPlus HR rule set (Table~\ref{tab:nhd-rules-abridged}; Figure~\ref{fig:nhd-workflow}) trades retention of every national-graph feature for a SWAT\textsuperscript{+}-legal routing tree; Table~\ref{tab:nhd-preprocessing-qa} quantifies that trade using original HU4 geodatabase zips versus exported project shapefiles (e.g., three of 56 original catchments consolidated for the small benchmark domain; 11 of 1{,}382 for the medium domain).
These rules target topological legality and auditability, not demonstrated hydrologic optimality.

\paragraph{Drainage-area fidelity.}
A complementary audit (Table~\ref{tab:drainage-area-audit}; \S\ref{sec:results-drainage-audit}) tests whether the executable model preserves cumulative drainage area, comparing \texttt{chandeg.con} channel areas with original NHDPlus HR \texttt{TotDASqKm} at gage-assigned channels across the eight catalog Model IDs: 82 of 97 matched gages (85\%) agree within $0.5$--$2.0\times$, and on the gage-dense Peace River domain the median SWAT\textsuperscript{+}/NHD ratio is 1.11 with assignment outliers reported separately.
Gage-to-channel assignment is itself auditable: an NHD-first/SWAT-second rule (Table~\ref{tab:station-assignment}) deliberately excludes SWAT\textsuperscript{+} area from the channel choice, and a two-way credibility check (Table~\ref{tab:drainage-credibility}) shows the NHD--USGS and SWAT\textsuperscript{+}--NHD agreement questions are near-independent ($r=-0.005$), so USGS catalog-area variance does not explain the model--hydrography offset.
A pipeline-stage trace (Table~\ref{tab:drainage-area-pipeline-trace}) localizes the systematic Peace-mainstem $+10$--$17\%$ excess: the original VAA, upstream catchment sums, pre-QSWAT\textsuperscript{+} reach attributes, and post-processed subbasin polygons all agree with the reference within $\pm3\%$, and the excess first appears at the QSWAT\textsuperscript{+}/TauDEM channel-area stage.
This is evidence that the NHDPlus HR preprocessing preserves cumulative drainage structure and that the residual offset is a delineation-definition difference introduced downstream---not a preprocessing error.
The live project channel area (\texttt{rivs1.AreaC} and SQLite \texttt{gis\_channels}) carries the full offset while the exported \texttt{chandeg.con} matches it to within $0.1\%$, confirming the offset is established in the QSWAT\textsuperscript{+}/TauDEM channel area ahead of a faithful SWAT\textsuperscript{+} export (Table~\ref{tab:drainage-area-pipeline-trace}, stage~H).

Objective 3 (structural and assembly cost). The S/M/L benchmark size classes span three orders of magnitude in HRU space; Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-sm-l-products} reports discretization counts, on-disk project size, and model-assembly wall time for the same Model IDs published in the runtime benchmark catalog.

Objective 4 (downstream workflow readiness). Gage basins \texttt{02297600} and \texttt{05536265} (Table~\ref{tab:model-roster}; Table~\ref{tab:controlled-gage-structure}) show that generated projects can complete initialization, PSO calibration, independent verification, and Morris batches on non-overlapping holdout periods.

\paragraph{What Morris demonstrates.}
Morris screening on the calibration window provides repeatable parameter perturbation, 999-member predictive bands with reported P-factor and R-factor (Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity-ensemble-metrics}; Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity-ensemble}), ranked $\mu^*$ sensitivities that differ between Florida (subsurface and lag parameters) and Illinois (snowmelt and channel routing among the largest effects), and evidence that ensemble machinery runs end-to-end on both hydroclimates.

\paragraph{What hydrologic metrics do not demonstrate.}
Split-sample NSE, PBIAS, and RMSE (Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:hydrologic-metrics}) characterize controlled-basin workflow diagnostics only.
They do not validate preprocessing fidelity, establish national predictive skill, or diagnose why Illinois holdout skill remained modest.
High Florida verification NSE is reported as a diagnostic, not as proof of platform success.

Objective 5 (simulation runtime; supporting evidence). On one documented host/build configuration, filtered print profiles dominate calibration-batch throughput on the small, medium, and large benchmark domains; the six-point ladder in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp-runtime-benchmark} shows that channel routing density can exceed HRU count as a scaling driver.

Table~\ref{tab:tool-contrast} provides \textbf{positioning context only} for this workflow relative to HAWQS \citep{Yen2016HAWQS} and BASINS \citep{EPABASINSFramework}.
HAWQS is documented around SWAT classic on medium-resolution NHDPlus V2 at HUC8--HUC12 resolutions; BASINS provides desktop GIS access with analyst-driven setup.

\subsection{Limitations}

\textbf{(1) Preprocessing hydrologic effects not isolated.}
The hydrography treatment in Section~\ref{sec:methods-nhd} prioritizes SWAT\textsuperscript{+}-compatible topology over retaining every national-graph feature.
This study documents and audits preprocessing choices but does not quantify uncertainty propagated from each preprocessing decision into hydrologic outputs or compare alternative rule configurations.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Divergence simplification. Reaches with divergence code~2 are removed; braided conveyance may require expert inspection of QA exports.
  \item Coastal and artificial flowlines. Near-ocean domains may require independent coastal checks.
  \item Isolated reaches. Dropped reaches are not promoted to standalone routed subbasins.
  \item Lakes and multi-outlet waterbodies. Single-primary-outlet simplification may require expert edits for complex reservoirs.
  \item Minimum subbasin area ($A_{\min}=62.5~\mathrm{km}^2$). The merge rule targets typical HUC12-scale units, not whole-domain coarsening; basins smaller than $A_{\min}$ can retain multiple subbasins when terminal units have no in-domain downstream merge target (small benchmark domain: 52.6~km$^2$, three subbasins). Isolated fragments below $A_{\min}/5$ may remain after nearest-neighbor connection.
  \item Raster--vector consistency. Terrain grids align to the preprocessed stream product; local mismatches remain possible.
  \item Dataset versioning. Authoritative version pins remain in the dataset inventory audit rows (Table~\ref{tab:data-master-abridged}; Supplementary Table~S1) before camera-ready submission.
\end{itemize}
The one-outlet partition, divergence-code-2 removal, lake simplification, and the $A_{\min}$ minimum-area rule are evaluated through structural counts and workflow execution, not through sensitivity analysis of alternative preprocessing configurations.

\textbf{(2) Workflow evaluation is not national validation.}
Controlled gage basins demonstrate that generated projects can enter standard calibration and Morris workflows; reported metrics do not establish CONUS-scale predictive skill.

\textbf{(3) Tool contrast is contextual only.}
Table~\ref{tab:tool-contrast} contrasts platform scope and hydrography resolution; it does not benchmark comparative hydrologic performance or batch runtime.

\textbf{(4) Generality limited to the evaluation matrix.}
Table~\ref{tab:model-roster} spans humid subtropical, semi-arid, and snow-influenced temperate domains in the eastern and western U.S.\ on selected catalog Model IDs.
Domains not represented include arid ephemeral systems, heavily regulated reservoir complexes, dense urban cores, and coastal tidal networks.
A CONUS-capable workflow is demonstrated on these selected domains only.

\textbf{(5) Drainage-area definition offset (characterized, not an error).}
The audit (\S\ref{sec:results-drainage-audit}) localizes the systematic $+10$--$17\%$ SWAT\textsuperscript{+}/NHD mainstem offset to the QSWAT\textsuperscript{+}/TauDEM channel-area computation: the live project channel area (\texttt{rivs1.AreaC} and SQLite \texttt{gis\_channels}) carries the full offset and the exported \texttt{chandeg.con} matches it to within $0.1\%$, so the SWAT\textsuperscript{+} export is faithful and the NHDPlus HR preprocessing is exonerated.
The residual offset is a TauDEM-contributing-area versus NHD-VAA delineation-definition difference rather than a defect; quantifying its hydrologic consequence (for example, on calibrated parameters) is left to future work.
Gage--channel assignment outliers on sparsely gaged domains are reported separately and warrant per-gage review before calibrating those stations.

\subsection{Professional judgment and decision support}

Automated assembly does not remove the modeler's responsibility for problem framing, parameter choices, calibration where hydrologic inference is required, or review of data licenses and uncertainties.
Readers should not treat any generated SWAT\textsuperscript{+} project as a substitute for professional judgment in regulatory or design filings without independent verification.

\subsection{Future work}

Phase-resolved cold-build timing (future work; documented in the archived evaluation materials) would further separate external acquisition from core processing for HUC-8 and huc12\_outlet extent modes beyond the production worker durations in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-sm-l-products}.
Expanded QA reporting tied to the full preprocessing rule catalog (Supplementary Table~S1) would ease peer review of preprocessing decisions.
Large-domain exported vectors would close remaining gaps in Table~\ref{tab:nhd-preprocessing-qa} post-extract counts.
