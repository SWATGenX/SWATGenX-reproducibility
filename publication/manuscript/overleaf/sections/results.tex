\section{Results}

\subsection{Overview and evidence tiers}
\label{sec:results-overview}

Results follow the three evidence tiers of Table~\ref{tab:objective-evidence-map}.
\textbf{Part~A} (\S\ref{sec:results-assembly-preprocessing}) is primary evidence on implementation auditability and hydrography preprocessing (Objectives~1--2); \textbf{Part~B} (\S\ref{sec:results-cal-val-sensitivity}) is secondary evidence on structural scaling and assembly cost (Objective~3) and downstream workflow readiness (Objective~4); \textbf{Part~C} (\S\ref{sec:results-runtime-benchmark}) is supporting evidence on simulation runtime (Objective~5).

\subsection{Assembly and preprocessing evidence}
\label{sec:results-assembly-preprocessing}

\subsubsection{Objective 1: Implementation and workflow architecture}
\label{sec:results-obj1}

The automated workflow produces a standard SWAT\textsuperscript{+} project tree---vector hydrography, gridded and station climate inputs, SQLite project tables, and optional QA sidecars---through the layered pipeline of Figure~\ref{fig:workflow}, with asynchronous workers (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture-layers}) separating long-running assembly from interactive use so outputs remain in a predictable, auditable per-basin layout (representative file classes in Table~\ref{tab:package-manifest-abridged}; full path patterns in Supplementary Table~S1). Structural footprints for the S/M/L size classes are in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-sm-l-products} (\S\ref{sec:results-obj3}).

\subsubsection{Objective 2: Hydrography preprocessing outcomes}
\label{sec:results-obj2}

National NHDPlus HR vectors are transformed into SWAT\textsuperscript{+}-compatible channel, subbasin, and lake layers through the staged pipeline of Figure~\ref{fig:nhd-workflow}; Table~\ref{tab:nhd-rules-abridged} lists the core rules and Table~\ref{tab:nhd-preprocessing-qa} compares the original USGS NHDPlus HR (HU4 geodatabase, domain-clipped) with the exported shapefiles for the S/M/L domains, documenting where divergence-code-2, isolated, and orphan/no-VAA catchments are consolidated (e.g.\ 56$\rightarrow$53 watershed polygons for S; 9{,}739 original catchments to 162 subbasins and 8{,}181 channels for L). These are structural-rule diagnostics, not demonstrated hydrologic benefit.

\subsubsection{Objective 2 (continued): Drainage-area fidelity audit}
\label{sec:results-drainage-audit}

Structural counts confirm the rules were applied but not that the \emph{executable} model preserves cumulative drainage structure. To close that gap, \texttt{chandeg.con} channel area was compared with original NHDPlus HR \texttt{TotDASqKm} at gage-assigned channels (\S\ref{sec:methods-drainage-audit}) across all eight Model IDs (Table~\ref{tab:drainage-area-audit}). Of 98 audited gages, 97 matched a SWAT\textsuperscript{+} channel and 82 (85\%) fell within the loose $0.5$--$2.0\times$ band. The band is deliberately wide: a gage can snap to a tributary, canal, or lake-outlet reach whose cumulative area differs sharply from the intended mainstem, so a factor-of-two band isolates true structural mismatch from discrete assignment error. Where assignment is least ambiguous---the gage-dense Peace River domain---the distribution tightens well inside the band: 66 of 76 gages agree, median ratio $1.11$, $10$th--$90$th percentile $0.78$--$1.28$ (central 80\% within $\approx\pm25\%$). Per-model medians span $0.57$--$1.16$, with the lowest values driven by a few assignment outliers on the sparsely gaged scaling domains rather than basin-wide conversion error.

\paragraph{Station--channel assignment and agreement (Peace River).}
The whole-HUC-8 Peace domain (\texttt{03100101}; 76 gages) supports per-class analysis. The NHD-first/SWAT-second method (Table~\ref{tab:station-assignment}) mapped 61 gages by exact NHD$\rightarrow$GIS crosswalk, 10 by lake-outlet replacement, and 5 by controlled downstream replacement, yielding 36 clean tributaries, 2 clean mainstem reaches, 16 mainstem reaches with a known NHD offset, and 22 lake/canal cases flagged for review; a reconciliation funnel left 16 fully calibration-ready gages (54 passed the assignment class, 20 with usable 2000--2024 daily streamflow). All 76 gages matched a SWAT\textsuperscript{+} channel (median ratio 1.11, 66 inside the loose band; Figure~\ref{fig:drainage-area-audit-scatter}). The NHD reference also tracks the independent USGS NWIS catalog (Table~\ref{tab:drainage-credibility}): of 57 gages with a site area, 43 agree within 25\% (median NHD/NWIS 0.85), and the two alignment questions are near-independent ($r=-0.005$), so catalog variance does not drive the model--hydrography offset (per-class detail in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp-delineation}).

\paragraph{Where the offset enters.}
The 16 mainstem-offset gages carry a systematic $+10$--$17\%$ excess in SWAT\textsuperscript{+} cumulative area. Tracing that area through the pipeline (Table~\ref{tab:drainage-area-pipeline-trace}) localizes it: the original VAA, upstream catchment-area sums, the pre-QSWAT\textsuperscript{+} cleaned-flowline attribute, and the post-processed watershed polygons all agree with the reference within $\pm3\%$ (median ratios $0.99$--$1.03$), while the excess first appears at the QSWAT\textsuperscript{+}/TauDEM channel-area stage (\texttt{AreaC}, ratio $\approx1.17$) and persists into \texttt{chandeg.con}. The live project channel area (\texttt{rivs1.AreaC} and SQLite \texttt{gis\_channels}) carries the full $+17\%$ and \texttt{chandeg.con} matches it to within $0.1\%$. The NHDPlus HR preprocessing therefore preserves cumulative drainage structure; the residual is a delineation-definition difference between TauDEM channel contributing area and NHD hydrosequence cumulative drainage, introduced downstream of the preprocessing this paper documents. These are structural-fidelity diagnostics, not hydrologic validation.

\subsection{Downstream workflow readiness}
\label{sec:results-cal-val-sensitivity}

\subsubsection{Objective 4: Workflow execution on controlled gage basins}
\label{sec:results-obj4}

Two controlled gage basins (\texttt{02297600} Florida, \texttt{05536265} Illinois), separate from the S/M/L set, test whether generated projects enter a standard downstream workflow (\S\ref{sec:methods-calibration}). On both, the platform completed initialization pools, PSO calibration, independent-period forward runs, and Morris batch execution with ensemble diagnostics on non-overlapping scored windows (Florida holdout 2019--2024; Illinois holdout 2012--2015; Table~\ref{tab:controlled-gage-structure}). These results do not isolate individual preprocessing effects or establish national validity.

\paragraph{Morris sensitivity and ensemble uncertainty.}
Morris analysis (1000 evaluations; 27 trajectories, four levels) on each basin's calibration window yields a 999-member 2.5--97.5\% predictive band (Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity-ensemble}; Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity-ensemble-metrics}); P-factor and R-factor characterize that band, not overall goodness-of-fit. Florida's ensemble is narrower (daily P-factor~0.71, R-factor~0.73) than Illinois (0.87, 3.13). Ranked $\mu^*$ (Tables~\ref{tab:morris-sensitivity}, \ref{tab:morris-sensitivity-05536265}) differ by climate: Florida is driven by \texttt{surq\_lag}, \texttt{perco}, \texttt{alpha\_bf}, and \texttt{cn3\_swf} with negligible snow terms, whereas Illinois is driven by snow (\texttt{melt\_min}, \texttt{melt\_max}) and channel routing (\texttt{k}, \texttt{mann}). That driver set \emph{explains} the weaker Illinois fit and wider band---snow forcing and routing are degenerate against streamflow alone---which is the intended use of these diagnostics: to surface where and why a generated model is hard to calibrate, not to certify skill (spider charts and monthly ensembles in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp-calval-sensitivity}).

\paragraph{Split-sample calibration diagnostics.}
Full daily/monthly NSE, RMSE, and PBIAS by stage are in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:hydrologic-metrics}; holdout skill is summarized here and is not interpreted as platform validation.
On \textbf{Florida} (\texttt{02297600}, channel~2; calibration 2013--2018 after a three-year warm-up, daily NWIS from 2009-10-01), the initialization pool best captured seasonal timing (daily NSE~0.80), calibration reached daily/monthly NSE~0.84/0.90, and independent verification retained 0.73/0.80 with modest wet bias (PBIAS~$\approx+7\%$).
On \textbf{Illinois} (\texttt{05536265}, channel~25; calibration 2020--2024, pre-calibration holdout 2012--2015), skill was lower throughout (calibration daily/monthly NSE~0.37/0.60; verification 0.22/0.42), reported as a workflow diagnostic on a second hydroclimate---automated assembly does not ensure calibration-ready hydrology, and Morris still completed (999 members) with the wider band noted above.

\subsubsection{Objective 5: SWAT\textsuperscript{+} simulation runtime benchmark}
\label{sec:results-obj5}

Objective~5 reports measured simulation cost on one documented host/build (AMD EPYC 7282, 10~vCPU; Intel ifx \texttt{-O3 -ipo}; calendar-2021 365-day runs under the filtered NetCDF \texttt{channel\_sd} profile). Filtered runs scale from about 5~s (473 HRUs) to about 100~s (11{,}284 HRUs) and about 23~min (94{,}303 HRUs) per simulated year (Table~\ref{tab:runtime-benchmark}; HRU scaling in Figure~\ref{fig:runtime-benchmark-hru-scaling}). Print-scope, export-format, compiler, and the six-point X20/X40/X60 ladder appear in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp-runtime-benchmark} and on the public benchmark page \citep{SWATGenXcompanion}.
